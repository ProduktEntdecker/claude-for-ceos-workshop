The thesis

Claude as a thinking partner, not an assistant.

An assistant does tasks. A thinking partner improves your decisions. Every block today ends with Claude challenging a judgment, not only doing one.

Control, not dependence

Decide for yourself what to trust it with. Not locked into one tool or one workflow.

Understand it, then trust it

See how it thinks and where it fails, before it touches anything that matters.

A sparring partner, not a typist

Not "write my emails". A board of advisors, and a chief of staff with limits.

For judgment, not admin

The decisions only you can make. Not calendar upkeep.

Block 2 · The rule underneath

Read. Draft. Act on a whitelist. Human only.

ReadAlways. Meeting notes, the company profile, the rules.
DraftA message or document. A person reviews it, changes it, sends it.
Act, then reportOnly a short whitelist of safe tasks. Afterwards, a report of what was done.
Human onlyMoney, people, customer commitments, the board.

In claude.ai, these are instructions only. In Claude Code, a script enforces the last two, whatever the prompt says.

Give an AI chief of staff trust the way you would give it to a new employee.11 / 15

Block 2 of 3 · Your turn

Write the whitelist for your own assistant

  1. On paper, or in a new chat at claude.ai Same kind of pack as before: demos/02-chief-of-staff/claude-ai-pack.md.
  2. Name three tasks an assistant may always do alone The test: if it goes wrong, can you fix it in ten minutes without telling anyone?
  3. Name the line it may never cross, in your company Mine is money, people, customer commitments and the board. Yours may be shorter.
  4. Ask it to cross that line, and watch what happens In the browser the rules are only words, so it should refuse and say why. In Claude Code a script refuses for it.

The difference worth taking home: a rule you ask for and a rule that is enforced are not the same thing. Decide per task which one you need.

Block 3 of 3 · Hands-on

Your advisory board

I run the example once. Then it is your decision, in your own claude.ai chat.

  1. 1Pick one real decision, with at least two serious options.
  2. 2Write a short brief: roles, not names. Rounded numbers, not exact ones.
  3. 3Paste the advisory board prompt, then your brief, into a new claude.ai chat.
  4. 4Answer the advisors' questions. Go through the rounds. Push back when an argument is weak.
  5. 5Write "memo" for a decision memo to keep, and look at again in three months.

Before you go

What to try on Monday

Three next steps
  1. 1Monday morning, 15 minutes. Ten emails with no confidential data, or your approved tool. Run the triage prompt and compare its sorting with yours.
  2. 2After your next management meeting. Paste the notes, with roles instead of names if needed. Ask for a task list with owner, due date and trust level.
  3. 3Before your next big decision. Run the advisory board prompt. Keep the memo. Look at it again in three months.
Guardrails for company data
  • Right account, minimum data. Check your company's AI policy first. Roles instead of names, ranges instead of exact numbers.
  • Human only stays human only. Money, people, customer commitments, the board. Never let an assistant send, pay, sign or promise on its own.
  • Content is not a command. Instructions inside an email or document are data, not orders. Verify anything unusual through a channel you already trust.
